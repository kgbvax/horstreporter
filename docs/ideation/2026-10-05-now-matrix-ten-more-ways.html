The Now matrix against its normal: ten more ways, and a control

5 Oct 2026, round two. Round one's pick (disc and ring) shipped as an experiment and was discarded. Brief for this round, from the signoff questions: the matrix must answer which band, where and what is unusual at one glance; the headline value is now against the normal for this hour; the geometry may leave the grid; web first. Every mockup renders from a live from-here /api/prop_intel/v2 payload for JO32 captured 22:53 UTC today (48 cells, each with its normal) and from the real 28-day diurnal normals of the JO32 block (dx_region_baseline_daily). Nothing here is implemented; this page is for signoff.

What the disc taught us

Three things went wrong, and all three are known results, not bad luck.

  • Area is the weakest quantitative channel. Cleveland and McGill's experiments rank position on a common scale first, then length, then angle, then area, then colour. A disc asks the eye to compare areas; a ring inside a disc asks it to compare two areas. Both are bottom-tier tasks at 10 px.
  • Five decades on one radius leaves no room for the signal. A log radius over 1 to 100,000 makes a doubling less than one unit of forty. The question we care about (now against normal) lives in a factor of 2 to 4, so it vanished.
  • Two concentric marks force a comparison; one mark that already is the comparison does not. Every proposal below encodes the ratio directly (as a position, a length, an angle, a count of pips, a word, a texture) and treats volume as a secondary weight, not the other way round.

Ground rules for this round

  • The quantity is expected_spots / expected, taken as log2 and clamped to ±3: ×⅛ to ×8, with ×1 in the middle. Halving and doubling are the same distance. Within ×0.7 to ×1.4 counts as normal.
  • No hue carries meaning. One ink per theme, plus the existing band colour on the row header. Anything that needs colour to be read is out (about 8% of men would read it wrong; printing kills it).
  • Volume (all-source spot count) stays visible as a weight: five decades as five steps of stroke, size or type weight.
  • A cell that usually has traffic at this hour but is silent now is a state worth showing. The backend dropped silent cells with the disc; five such cells exist in this payload (17m NA normally 179, 15m NA 43, 30m JA 32, 40m JA 10, 60m CAR 5). Proposals mark them where they can.
  • Mode badges shrink to one convention: solid means SSB is workable, hollow or dotted means CW only.

0. Numbers only (the control)

The simplest honest answer: type the factor. "×2.3" needs no legend. Type weight and size follow the distance from normal, so the page scans like a newspaper: large bold figures are the news, light small ones are the weather. Every other proposal has to beat this.

1. Bullet bars

Stephen Few's bullet graph, one per cell: a bar for now, a tick for the normal, both on the same five-decade log scale. The eye reads the bar against the tick without a legend, and the part of the bar past the tick is set in full ink so the excess pops; a shortfall shows as a dotted gap up to the tick.

Channel
Length against a reference mark (Cleveland rank 2)
Grid
unchanged
Backend
none
Risk
Log length still compresses the factor; the excess/shortfall ink rescues it

2. Deviation stems

Each band row becomes a diverging chart around its own normal line. A stem rises for above normal and drops for below, length is the log factor, the dot's size is the volume. The row reads as a skyline of surprises; a flat row is a normal evening. Columns stay put, so "who reaches JA" is still a glance down one column.

Channel
Position and length from a shared baseline (rank 1 to 2)
Grid
kept; the cell border fades, the row rule carries the structure
Backend
none; silent cells need silent=1
Risk
Needs 40 px of row height; tight on phones

3. Dot strip

Cleveland's dot plot: one shared axis from ×⅛ to ×8, one row per band, every region a labelled dot placed where it stands against normal. Regions give up their fixed column, but gain a name in the mark, so no legend and no header lookup. Anything right of the centre line is better than usual; a crowd on the line is an ordinary night.

Channel
Position on a common scale (rank 1)
Grid
rows stay bands; columns dissolve into an axis
Backend
none; silent cells need silent=1
Risk
Labels collide when several regions are normal; the mock alternates label sides

4. Doubling pips

Seven pips per cell, like a signal meter you can count. The middle pip lights for any traffic; one filled pip to the right for every doubling above normal, one ring to the left for every halving below. ×4 is two dots to the right. Discrete, countable, draws with primitives a home-screen widget already has.

Channel
Count of marks plus direction (ordinal, exact to one doubling)
Grid
unchanged
Backend

Risk
Volume is only a faint underline; "many pips" could be misread as "busy" until learned

5. Tilt field

One stroke per cell. Upright is normal; it leans right as the cell runs above normal, left as it falls below, up to 60° at ×8 and ×⅛. Stroke weight is the volume. The matrix becomes a field of needles and the eye reads the lean of a whole row or column at once, the way a slope field or a wind chart is read.

Channel
Angle (rank 3), weight for volume

Angle is less precise than length; it is the most distinctive look here, and the most likely to be called a gimmick

6. Day strip

Each cell draws this path's whole typical day as a small shaded curve (the 28-day normal, hour by hour), a cursor at the current UTC hour, and a dot for right now. The dot above the curve means better than usual; its place on the curve says whether the opening is just starting, at its peak, or closing. No other proposal answers "should I hurry?".

Channel
Position in time, position against a curve (small multiples)
Grid
unchanged
Backend
24 hourly normals per cell (one query, cached per area per day)
Risk
Dense at 44 px; the curve must be a silhouette, not a chart

7. Sparkline against normal

Tufte's sparkline: the last hour in 5-minute bins, with the normal as a dashed line. "Rising" stops being a chevron and becomes a visible slope; a surge is a line that leaves its dashed normal behind. Reads at a glance as a trend, and rewards a second look with the shape.

Channel
Position over time against a reference line
Grid
unchanged
Backend
per-cell 5-minute bins from the hub window (cheap; the mock's series are illustrative, ending at the real count)
Risk
Twelve points at 40 px is near the floor; must be a line, not an area

8. MUF skyline

The grid, read as a ham reads it. Bands are stacked by frequency, highest on top; regions run west to east like a world strip, with their beam headings. A rule sits on the highest band open to each region: that is the ceiling, your working MUF estimate, drawn as a skyline across the world. Above the rule nothing happens; below it dots say how each band compares to normal.

Channel
Position (the ceiling line), size for volume, ink weight for factor
Grid
kept, rows inverted, columns reordered by longitude
Backend
none
Risk
A single stray spot lifts the ceiling; needs the 5-spot floor

9. Compass board

Leave the grid entirely. Regions sit around your station by beam heading, north up, as on the azimuthal map. Each tile lists the bands open to that region as pills carrying their factor; bold pills are above normal, dashed ones usually work at this hour but are silent now. Turn the antenna with your eye, then pick a band.

Channel
Layout by bearing, type weight for factor, band colour on the pill edge
Grid
replaced
Backend
none; silent cells need silent=1
Risk
Column scans across bands are gone; taller than the grid on a phone

10. Hatching

Bertin's texture variables, which print and survive any colour vision: line spacing is the volume, line direction is the verdict. Rising diagonals for above normal, falling for below, horizontal for normal, cross-hatch for a surge, dotted lines for CW only. It looks like an engraving and it is read like one: dense and climbing is good news.

Channel
Texture density (volume) and orientation (verdict)
Grid
unchanged
Backend
none; silent cells need silent=1
Risk
Moiré on low-DPI screens; direction is categorical, so ×2 and ×8 look alike

Recommendation

Default: 2, Deviation stems, with 0 as its tooltip. It uses the two strongest perceptual channels, keeps every column where it is, needs no colour and no legend beyond "up is better than usual", and the surge and the silent states fall out of the same mark. Pair it with the numbers from 0 in the hover and the exact figures are one pointer away.

Second view: 6, Day strip. It is the only proposal that answers a question the operator actually asks at 22:50 UTC: is 20m to NA closing or is this a lull. It needs one backend addition (24 hourly normals per cell, which the almanac tables already hold) and would sit behind the existing look chips, replacing Inferno.

Worth a prototype, not the default: 8 (MUF skyline) is the most ham-shaped idea on the page and costs almost nothing, but the ceiling line is only as honest as the 5-spot floor. 9 (Compass board) is the right shape for the phone app, where the grid never fit. 5 and 10 are the two surprises; both read well on this data and both would need a week of real use to know if they stay legible. 3 lost its columns, which the brief said it may, but it answers "where" worse than the grid did.
